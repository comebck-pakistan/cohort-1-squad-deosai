**1. Idea Recap**

**Idea:** Idea 8 --- The Social-Commerce Support Agent (Cohort 1 Product
Challenge)

**Target user:** A Pakistani social seller running a fashion or
jewellery shop on Instagram or WhatsApp, managing every customer DM
alone, with little to no team and no automation.

**Core problem:** Repetitive DM questions (price, delivery,
availability, return policy) eat 30 minutes to 2 hours a day, messages
go unanswered after hours, and sellers waste time chasing COD orders
that get ghosted on delivery day.

**8-week wedge:** An Instagram/WhatsApp DM AI tool that answers the five
most common customer questions using the seller\'s own catalogue and
policies, and auto-sends a COD confirmation message when an order is
placed.

**Validation so far:** 12 sellers surveyed directly, plus 4 in-depth
interviews (one overlapping with the survey) --- 15 unique sellers
engaged in total. A brand processing 400--500 orders/day is still
handling everything manually with zero after-hours support.

**2. Methodology**

Two research tracks were run in parallel and are combined in this
document:

-   Structured survey --- a Google Form (\"Help Us Understand Customer
    Support Challenges for Instagram/WhatsApp Businesses\") sent to
    social sellers, covering platform, inquiry volume, common questions,
    time spent replying, lost-sale incidents, current tools, perceived
    usefulness, willingness to pay, and interest in early testing. 12
    responses collected between June 27--29, 2026.

-   In-depth interviews --- a structured interview guide covering the
    same themes in more depth, conducted with 4 sellers: StickerMania
    (Khadija Qureshi), AP Crafts, Jewellery with Maheen, and ZayZaiwar
    (Shamoon).

*Note: AP Crafts appears in both tracks (completed the form directly,
then elaborated by chat) --- so the two tracks together represent 15
unique sellers, not 16.*

**4. User Interview Summaries**

**4.1 StickerMania --- Khadija Qureshi**

**Business:** Stickersmania --- customizable stationery, stickers,
bookmarks, books, notepads. Running 3--4 years.

**Channels:** Instagram, WhatsApp, Facebook, and Daraz.

**Inquiry volume:** \~10 customer messages per month; mostly price
queries despite a catalogue already existing.

**Current process:** Khadija manages the page herself and replies within
a day or two.

**Lost sale?:** Yes --- lost 2 customers after missing an Instagram DM
notification. She doesn\'t check the account often unless posting or
replying, so the buyer assumed the page was inactive and never returned.

**Automation used:** Never felt the need for a bot even after losing
customers, but now checks the account more frequently as a workaround.
Does use WhatsApp Quick Replies.

**What she\'d automate first:** Pricing responses --- repetitive, and
price DMs sometimes crowd out DMs from customers actually ready to
order.

**Early access interest:** Yes, confirmed she\'s open to testing an
early version.

*Takeaway: The clearest signal in the batch --- a validated case of a
missed-notification lost sale, a named repetitive task (pricing) she
wants automated first, and explicit interest in testing.*

**4.2 AP Crafts**

**Business:** Custom craft pieces --- most orders are
bespoke/made-to-request rather than off-the-shelf items.

**Participation:** Completed the squad\'s intake form directly, then
elaborated further over chat specifically on the automation questions.

**Core objection:** Believes automation is largely useless for her
specific business because customers overwhelmingly want custom pieces
--- a bot can\'t negotiate a one-off custom order the way a human can.

**Reaction to a narrower bot scope:** When offered a smaller scope ---
closed/hours message, return-to-office time, delivery policy,
return/exchange policy, basic FAQs without waiting for the owner --- she
agreed it could help, but questioned whether it meaningfully reduces her
workload and whether it\'s worth the cost.

**Key concern:** Automation removes the \"human touch,\" which she
considers essential for smaller brands like hers.

**Early access interest:** Not firmly confirmed in the interview; on the
survey form she marked \"Maybe\" for testing.

*Takeaway: The clearest counter-signal in the batch. Custom/bespoke
sellers are a weaker fit for a general Q&A bot --- the wedge\'s five
questions (price, delivery, availability, returns, hours) mostly assume
a fixed catalogue. AP Crafts\' own suggestion (a lightweight
closed/hours/policy responder) hints at a narrower, cheaper entry point
for this segment rather than the full assistant.*

**4.3 Jewellery with Maheen**

**Business:** Handmade jewellery --- bracelets, earrings, mobile charms,
necklaces, cat collars, and occasional bag charms. Each piece is made to
order: she shows customers her bead selection and they choose what to
have made. Running since November 2022 (\~4 years, per respondent).

**Inquiry volume:** Low and uneven --- by her own framing, a small
business like hers might get 2 orders in a week, or sometimes none in a
month.

**Current process:** A customer sends a photo asking if a design is
\"available.\" She greets them, confirms availability and price, then
states her fixed rules: free delivery in Lahore, minimum order of Rs.
500, and advance payment required. Once the customer agrees, she shares
her bank account, waits for the transfer, and only then starts making
the piece --- advance payment protects her from customers cancelling
custom orders mid-production.

**Reply time:** About an hour.

**Lost sale?:** Never happened --- she has not lost a customer to a late
reply. She\'s dispatched 50+ parcels with zero returns.

**Automation choice:** Strongly in favor --- described a bot as \"really
needed\" for answering FAQs, product details, and availability so she
can spend less time on repetitive replies. Specifically wants it to tell
customers when the business is closed/back, and communicate her standard
rules automatically.

**Adjacent pain point (not core to the DM bot):** Flagged that many
small jewellery businesses, herself included, struggle to reliably
source raw materials/beads online --- a supply-side issue outside this
product\'s scope, but useful context on the seller\'s overall workload.

**Early access interest:** Yes --- confirmed she\'ll try an early
version.

*Takeaway: The strongest positive data point in the batch. Despite low
volume, she wants the automation specifically because it removes
repetitive availability/FAQ replies from her plate --- and notably, her
\~1-hour reply time correlates with zero lost sales, unlike slower-reply
sellers in this batch.*

**4.4 ZayZaiwar --- Shamoon**

**Business:** Previously sold jewellery, clothes, and other trending
products; has now paused and pivoted to homemade frozen items. Running
3--4 years. Interview scoped to the jewellery line specifically, since
that\'s this product\'s target category.

**Inquiry volume:** Not a fixed number --- volume depends on whether ads
are running.

**Most frequent questions:** Availability first (customers want to see
different articles/pieces), then price.

**Most frustrating part:** Even after being sent to the
website/catalogue, customers still ask one by one whether a specific
article is available --- jewellery was, in his words, the hardest
product line to manage this way.

**Current process:** Handles it entirely himself using Facebook Business
Manager; reply time runs 30 minutes to an hour.

**Experience with current tools:** Good experience overall, but believes
managing a large audience really needs multiple people, not just better
manual tools.

**Automation choice:** Automate the initial step of showing/confirming
available products --- the exact repetitive availability check that
frustrated him most.

**Early access interest:** Open to being recontacted once an MVP is
ready to trial; didn\'t give a firm commitment either way.

*Takeaway: Independently arrives at the same conclusion as Maheen ---
availability/product-lookup is the single most repetitive, most
requested-to-automate task --- while also surfacing a scaling insight:
past a certain inquiry volume, sellers feel they need more headcount,
which is exactly the gap automation can fill instead.*

**5. Cross-Cutting Findings (Survey + Interviews Combined)**

**5.1 Recurring pain points**

-   Availability and price questions dominate, even when a catalogue,
    website, or Business Manager listing already exists --- confirmed by
    8 of 12 survey respondents naming Product Questions as their first
    automation priority, and independently by StickerMania and ZayZaiwar
    in interviews.

-   Repetitive questions (price, delivery, availability, returns, hours)
    consume real time and, for higher-volume sellers, feel like they
    require more headcount rather than better tooling (ZayZaiwar; also
    visible in the survey, where the two highest-volume respondents both
    spend 1--4 hours/day replying).

-   After-hours / missed-notification gaps directly cost sales:
    StickerMania\'s case is a concrete lost-sale incident tied to a
    missed Instagram DM notification, and the survey shows 83% of
    respondents have lost sales at least occasionally to slow replies.

-   Manual, single-owner reply processes are the norm --- 11 of 12
    survey respondents reply entirely themselves, and all four
    interviewees run their own DMs with no dedicated support team.

**5.2 Reply speed vs. lost sales --- the clearest signal**

-   Maheen replies in \~1 hour and reports zero lost sales in 4 years
    and 50+ dispatched parcels.

-   Shamoon replies in 30 minutes to an hour and did not report a
    lost-sale incident.

-   StickerMania replies within a day or two and explicitly lost 2
    customers to a missed-notification delay.

-   This lines up with the survey-wide pattern (83% have lost sales at
    least \"rarely\") and with the wedge\'s core thesis: the sellers who
    reply fastest lose the fewest sales, and
    after-hours/instant-response coverage is exactly what the bot is
    meant to provide.

**5.3 Where sellers push back**

-   Perceived loss of \"human touch,\" especially for small/boutique or
    fully custom brands (AP Crafts) --- echoed faintly by the 1 survey
    respondent who rated the tool \"Not Useful.\"

-   Automation is a weaker fit where the product itself is
    bespoke/made-to-order (AP Crafts, and to a lesser extent Maheen) ---
    the value shifts from \"answer the question\" to \"triage before the
    human takes over.\"

-   Uncertainty about ROI/cost relative to actual workload reduction for
    lower-volume sellers --- reflected in the survey by the \"Maybe\"
    answers being the modal response for both willingness-to-pay (7
    of 12) and testing interest (8 of 12), rather than firm commitments.

**5.4 What this means for the wedge**

-   Availability/product-lookup and pricing are the two most-requested
    first features --- named by 8 of 12 survey respondents and
    independently by StickerMania, Maheen, and ZayZaiwar in interviews.
    Strong convergent validation for the wedge\'s scope.

-   Positioning should reassure sellers the tool handles repetitive
    triage, not the relationship-building part of the conversation ---
    directly answering the AP Crafts objection.

-   Segment by product type as well as volume: catalogue-driven sellers
    (StickerMania, ZayZaiwar, and most of the 12 survey respondents) are
    a strong fit for the full five-question assistant;
    custom/made-to-order sellers (AP Crafts, partly Maheen) are a better
    fit for a lighter closed-hours/policy/FAQ responder that hands off
    to the human once a custom conversation starts.

-   High-volume, ad-driven sellers (ZayZaiwar, the survey\'s 50--100
    msgs/day respondent, and the 400--500 orders/day brand from the
    original submission) feel the headcount pain most acutely --- likely
    the strongest near-term paying segment.

-   Rs. 500--1,000/month is the price point most sellers converge on (7
    of 12 in the survey) --- a realistic anchor for an early pricing
    tier, with room for a higher tier (Rs. 2,500--5,000) for
    higher-volume sellers.
